Prompt

We are simulating a toy economy in an undergraduate finance class. There are N companies, each producing one product. In a normal year every company's stock returns 20%. Two kinds of risk. Each year, independently across companies, each company's product succeeds with probability 30%, and that company's stock return is 40 percentage points higher that year, or fails with probability 30%, and that company's stock return is 40 percentage points lower that year. Each year, with probability 10%, something bad hits the whole economy, like a pandemic, and every company's stock return is 30 percentage points lower that year. Write a short, self-contained Python script (numpy and matplotlib only) with a function that simulates the stock returns of all N companies over a given number of years, using a fixed random seed. First, simulate 10 years for N = 3 and plot a grouped bar chart of the three companies' returns year by year, one group of three bars per year. Choose the seed so that these 10 years include at least one year where exactly one company's product succeeds and none fails, at least one year where exactly one company's product fails and none succeeds, and at least one year where a pandemic hits the whole economy, so I can point at those years in the chart. Second, an investor spreads the money equally across the N companies, so the investor's return in a year is the average of the N stock returns. Simulate 10,000 years for N = 1 and plot a histogram of the investor's annual return, printing its average and its standard deviation. Then do the same for N = 2, 5, 10, 30, and 100, print the standard deviation for each N, and plot the standard deviation against N as a line with markers. Do not compute any numbers yourself and do not guess what the results will look like, give me only the code.
